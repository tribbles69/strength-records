What a record means here

This is a registry, not a federation. There are no referees in the room, no calibrated plates and no drug testing. A record here is video-verified: it claims exactly what its video shows and nothing more. Nobody pays to submit or to hold a record, and categories are never sold.

General rules

  1. One rep. Records are single lifts. The two holds are a fixed 10 seconds.
  2. Kilograms. Records are kept in kilograms. Pounds are accepted and converted, rounded down to the nearest 0.5 kg.
  3. The weight is the bar plus plates plus collars, as shown on the video. Say what the bar weighs. Specialty bars need their weight stated.
  4. Breaking a record takes at least 0.5 kg more. A tie does not take the record: the earlier lift keeps it.
  5. Allowed on every lift: belt, chalk or liquid chalk, wrist wraps, knee sleeves, any footwear or none.
  6. Never allowed: squat or deadlift suits, bench shirts, knee wraps, elbow wraps or sleeves on pressing lifts, bands or chains on the bar, tacky or other adhesives, and anyone touching the bar or the lifter before the lift is finished.
  7. Straps are allowed only where the lift's standard says so. They must be ordinary lifting straps.
  8. The moderator's call. If something on the video is doubtful, the moderator can ask for a re-film or decline the lift, and gives the reason.

Divisions

Every table is kept twice: Everyone and Women. You say which you are entering. A lift that qualifies for more than one division or table can be entered in each.

Adaptive / para

You choose a category and describe your adaptation in a sentence or two, in your own words. Both are shown on the record, because that is what lets people compare like with like. You decide how much to say. No medical proof is asked for.

    A standard is applied as far as your body allows, and the adaptation is stated on the record: lockout to your own end range, a seated or strapped-in setup, a prosthesis, a hook, a one-arm variation, a different start position. Declare it, show it, and the lift is judged against what you declared. Equipment that is part of the adaptation is allowed and is listed on the record.

    Adaptive records are kept per lift and per category. Bodyweight is shown if you give it, but these tables are not split by bodyweight class.

    Bodyweight classes

    The upper limit of each class is included. The class is decided by the number on the scale, with no allowance for clothing.

      Bodyweight is proved in one of two ways:

      • Same take (preferred). Step on a scale in the same unedited video as the lift, with the reading legible. First show the scale reading zero, then reading a marked plate (a 20 kg plate reading 20, say), so the scale itself is checked.
      • Same day. A separate unedited video filmed the same day, showing the same scale check and the weigh-in. The record is labelled "same day" so readers can see which it was.

      No weigh-in on video? The lift can still go in Open.

      Open

      Anyone, any bodyweight, no weigh-in. The heaviest verified lift holds the record. If you give your bodyweight without a weigh-in it is shown as self-reported.

      What the video must show

      1. One unedited take. A single continuous shot from showing the weight to the end of the lift. No cuts, speed changes, filters, or cropping that hides part of you or the bar. Original sound helps.
      2. The weight, on camera. Before the lift and in the same take, show both ends of the bar: every plate with its markings readable, the collars and the bar. For a rack pull, show the tape measure against the bar.
      3. The whole lift in frame. Your whole body and the bar, from start to finish, from the angle given in that lift's standard.
      4. A public link. You host the video. YouTube is preferred (public or unlisted) because it plays on the record page. Instagram links are accepted and show as a button.
      5. It is you. You are the lifter, or you have the lifter's permission. Lifters must be 18 or over.
      6. The video stays up. If it is deleted or made private the record is marked "evidence unavailable", and removed if it is not restored within 30 days.
      7. Plates do not need to be calibrated, but must be commercially made and marked. Home-made, unmarked or altered plates are not accepted.

      History, disputes and removal

      Beaten records stay. When a record is broken the old one is marked "superseded" and stays on the page with its video.

      Anyone can dispute a record using the dispute form. Say which record, what is wrong and where in the video. Valid grounds: the lift does not meet the written standard, the weight shown does not match the claim, the video is edited, the bodyweight or adaptive declaration is false, or the lifter is not who was claimed. "That looks fake" with nothing specific is not a ground.

      A disputed record shows "under review" and keeps its place while it is checked. The lifter is contacted if they left an email and has 14 days to answer. It is then either upheld or removed.

      Disputes about the moderator's own lifts are decided by a second moderator, who will be named here before launch.

      A record is removed when a dispute is upheld against it, when its video has been unavailable for 30 days, when it turns out to break the video rules, or when the lifter asks. A record removed for cause leaves a line in the history showing the date, the weight and the reason, without the lifter's name. A record removed at the lifter's request is deleted completely.
